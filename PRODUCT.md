# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary users are authors working in the authenticated studio to create, organize, edit, theme, and publish poetry collections, poems, novels, and chapters. Their central workflow is writing and managing literary work efficiently while shaping each collection into a distinctive visual world with authored or AI-generated backgrounds.

Readers use the public surface to discover and read published work. They do not configure the experience; they encounter the atmosphere, typography, and accent treatment chosen by the author for each collection.

## Product Purpose

Dreamer’s Palette is a creator-first literary publishing studio paired with an immersive public reader. It helps authors move from writing to publication without fighting a generic CMS, while giving them controlled visual tools that make each collection feel unique.

Success means authors can remain focused and productive throughout creation, organization, theming, preview, and publishing, and readers receive a calm, legible, emotionally resonant experience that changes meaningfully with each collection.

## Brand Personality

Calm, literary, creator-first. The dashboard should feel focused and capable without becoming clinical. The public reader should feel atmospheric and intimate while keeping the writing—not the interface or generated imagery—as the primary presence.

## Anti-references

- Generic CMS dashboards that reduce literary work to undifferentiated forms and tables.
- Flashy AI generators that make generation the spectacle or obscure what will be produced.
- Social publishing platforms built around feeds, engagement metrics, and attention capture.
- Decorative interfaces that compete with the writing through excessive cards, gradients, glass effects, motion, or saturated chrome.
- Atmospheric themes that sacrifice contrast, legibility, or navigation clarity.

The current application is a valid identity baseline. Future work should refine its hierarchy, consistency, responsiveness, and accessibility rather than replacing its overall calm literary character.

## Design Principles

1. Keep authors in creative flow. Common writing, previewing, organizing, and publishing actions should remain direct, predictable, and low-friction.
2. Let authors shape atmosphere without becoming designers. Theming and AI-assisted imagery should offer clear choices, immediate previews, and safe readability defaults.
3. Make the writing the strongest signal. Interface chrome and imagery support the work and recede when reading begins.
4. Connect studio decisions to reader outcomes. Authors should understand how collection settings affect the public experience before publishing.
5. Prefer earned familiarity over novelty. Standard product affordances, consistent components, and clear state feedback take priority over decorative invention.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Follow standard modern accessibility practices, targeting WCAG AA where practical. Maintain keyboard access, visible focus states, semantic controls, sufficient text and UI contrast, readable type and line lengths, useful labels and error messages, and reduced-motion behavior. Collection themes and generated backgrounds must preserve legibility across text modes and viewport sizes rather than relying on authors to detect contrast failures manually.
